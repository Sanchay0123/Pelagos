# Commercial Freight Route Intelligence Specification (ROUT-006)

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the canonical technical specification for Milestone **D9.5 Commercial Freight Route Intelligence** within the Pelagos platform.

The purpose of D9.5 is to bridge the verified physical maritime routing foundation established in D9.4 ([ROUT-004](./D9.4-stage-3-design.md), [ROUT-005](./D9.4-increment-3-gui-integration.md)) with commercial freight market intelligence. While D9.4 resolves navigable sea-lane paths, canal transits, and nautical distances using Eurostat SeaRoute MARNET topology, D9.5 attaches commercial meaning to those voyages: resolving whether a route represents an actively monitored commercial trade corridor, retrieving point-in-time verified historical freight rate observations, providing deterministic baseline rate forecasts, strictly disclaiming vessel charter availability from AIS telemetry alone, and suppressing fabricated commercial guidance on unmonitored trade lanes.

This specification defines the boundary between physical hydrography and commercial freight analytics, enforces strict truth-in-data principles, and formalizes the interfaces between `pelagos.routing`, `pelagos.forecasting`, `pelagos.storage`, and the user-facing command center UI.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 In-Scope Capabilities

1. **Deterministic Endpoint & Corridor Resolution:**
   - Snapping origin and destination query endpoints (port codes, coordinate tuples, or geographic dictionaries) to verified maritime port fairways within a bounded commercial endpoint-to-port matching radius ($\le 150.0\text{ NM}$, as implemented in `backend/pelagos/routing/corridors.py`). This macro-regional commercial matching radius is strictly distinct from the $50.0\text{ NM}$ hydrographic sea-lane entrance-gate snapping radius established in Milestone D9.4 ([ROUT-004](./D9.4-stage-3-design.md)).
   - Resolving resolved port endpoints to trade regions via an authoritative mapping dictionary.
   - Deterministic matching of regional port-pairs against registered commercial trade corridors.
   - Explicit enforcement of directional asymmetry (e.g., recognizing outbound export corridors while rejecting unmonitored inbound backhauls).
2. **Point-in-Time Observed Rate Attribution:**
   - Querying and exposing the latest genuine historical freight rate observation for monitored corridors from dual-tier SQLite / Parquet storage.
   - Retaining complete provenance metadata: publication date, observation date, currency (`USD`), unit of measure (`USD/MT`), source attribution, dataset identifier, and distinction between published benchmarks and derived indicators.
3. **Route-Conditioned Baseline Rate Forecasting:**
   - Generating single-horizon point forecasts (implemented for $T+1$ month forward horizon) for monitored corridors using deterministic, explainable statistical baseline models: Persistence, 3-Month Moving Average (MA-3), 6-Month Moving Average (MA-6), and 6-Month Linear Trend Extrapolation (Trend-6 with non-negative clamping). Clarification: The notations MA-3 and MA-6 specify historical trailing observation window lengths ($k=3, k=6$ months) and do not denote 3-month or 6-month forward forecast horizons.
   - Strict enforcement of zero future-data lookahead by fencing historical observation windows to the forecast origin date.
4. **Honest Unmonitored Suppression & Zero-Fabrication:**
   - Returning explicit `unmonitored` status for valid navigable maritime routes that do not map to monitored commercial freight corridors (e.g., Rotterdam $\rightarrow$ Singapore).
   - Completely suppressing rate values, forecast values, and index points on unmonitored routes, preventing the generation of synthetic or fabricated commercial guidance.
5. **Strict Boundary Between Telemetry & Charter Availability:**
   - Enforcing an architectural boundary between raw AIS vessel positions and commercial charter readiness.
   - Returning `status: "unavailable"` for vessel availability with explicit notices that commercial open-tonnage lists are unconnected.
6. **Unified Route Intelligence REST API & GUI Integration:**
   - Serving combined corridor, observation, baseline forecast, vessel availability, and scope disclaimer payloads via `/api/v1/freight/route-intelligence`.
   - Coordinated rendering within the Pelagos command center panel (`pelagosPanel.js`) and directions layer (`layers/directions/index.js`).

### 2.2 Explicitly Out-of-Scope Capabilities

1. **Global Commercial Freight Rate Coverage:** Pelagos makes no claim of universal commercial freight monitoring. Commercial rate coverage is strictly bounded to registered corridors with genuine historical fixture data.
2. **High-Frequency (Daily or Weekly) Rate Series:** The verified USDA AMS baseline dataset is strictly monthly. Daily or sub-monthly forecasting horizons ($7, 15, 30\text{ days}$) are out of scope for the current data foundation.
3. **Advanced Machine Learning / Econometric Regression Models:** Multi-factor regression models (XGBoost, gradient boosting, neural networks, LSTM) incorporating macro variables (bunker fuel prices, commodity prices, fleet deadweight capacity) are deferred to future milestones.
4. **Calibrated Prediction Intervals at Runtime:** Upper and lower confidence bounds (p10/p90, 95% intervals) are not implemented in the current API response schema and are out of scope for the initial D9.5 baseline.
5. **Commercial Vessel Chartering & Laycan Matching (Milestone D10):** Physical laycan candidate matching, charterparty contract evaluation, voyage profit-and-loss optimization, and vessel fixture negotiation belong strictly to Milestone D10.
6. **Non-Bulk Commodities:** Container freight rates (TEU spot rates, SCFI), tanker spot rates (Worldscale, Baltic Clean/Dirty Tanker Indices), and specialized breakbulk rates are explicitly out of scope.
7. **Synthetic Data Generation / Interpolation:** Synthesizing unobserved months or fabricating missing route rates is strictly prohibited ([REQ-NFR-DIP-001](../requirements/nonfunctional-requirements.md)).
8. **Proprietary Third-Party AI Services:** OpenAI, Anthropic, or external cloud LLM dependencies are strictly prohibited ([REQ-NFR-DCT-001](../requirements/nonfunctional-requirements.md), [ADR-0004](../architecture/adr/adr-0004-core-technology-independence.md)).

## 3. Stakeholders and User-Facing Outcomes

### 3.1 Primary Stakeholders

- **Maritime Charterers & Cargo Traders:** Require transparent freight rate benchmarks and explainable baseline forecasts for specific agricultural export corridors to inform voyage timing and contract selection.
- **Freight Risk Analysts:** Require uncompromised data provenance, explicit disclaimers regarding commodity boundaries, and zero-lookahead backtest transparency.
- **Vessel Operations Planners:** Need combined navigational sea-lane metrics (nautical miles, canal transits, waypoints) with commercial corridor context.
- **Project Overseer & System Auditors:** Require strict adherence to truth-in-status principles, zero synthetic data, and verifiable test coverage.

### 3.2 User-Facing Outcomes

1. **When calculating a route on a monitored commercial corridor (e.g., Seattle $\rightarrow$ Yokohama):**
   - The user sees the 3D navigable sea-lane polyline draped on the globe (4,512.3 NM).
   - The user concurrently receives the Commercial Freight Dashboard displaying:
     - **Corridor Tag:** `MONITORED` with corridor name (`U.S. Pacific Northwest to Japan Bulk Grain Ocean Corridor`) and code (`PNW-JPN-BULK`).
     - **Observed Rate:** Latest verified rate (illustrative fixture-specific sample value: $37.31\text{ USD/MT}$ as of sample observation date `2026-09-01`), source attribution (`USDA AMS / O'Neil Commodity Consulting`), and indicator that the rate is a verified benchmark (not derived). *(Note: Rate values and dates are illustrative fixture-specific samples from the test dataset, not invariant contractual constants).*
     - **Forecast Rate:** 1-month persistence baseline (illustrative fixture-specific sample value: $37.31\text{ USD/MT}$ for sample target date `2026-10-01`, $T+1$ month horizon).
     - **Scope Disclaimers:** Explicit disclosure that rates apply specifically to agricultural bulk grain in Panamax/Supramax carriers.
     - **Fleet Notice:** Explicit disclosure that live AIS telemetry does not establish commercial vessel charter availability.
2. **When calculating a route on an unmonitored maritime corridor (e.g., Rotterdam $\rightarrow$ Singapore):**
   - The user sees the full navigable sea-lane polyline through Suez and Gibraltar (8,409.8 NM).
   - The Commercial Freight Dashboard displays:
     - **Corridor Tag:** `UNMONITORED`.
     - **Notice:** Clear explanation that the Northern Europe to Southeast Asia trade lane is unmonitored in Pelagos.
     - **Suppression:** Rate and forecast fields are completely hidden; no fabricated rates or global averages are displayed.

## 4. Domain Terminology and Route Identity

### 4.1 Definitions

- **Navigable Maritime Route:** A hydrographically verified sea-lane path connecting two coordinates on a navigable graph (e.g., Eurostat MARNET 50 km network), characterized by nautical distance, waypoints, and canal transit rules.
- **Commercial Freight Corridor:** An established commercial trade lane connecting an export origin region with an import destination region, associated with published freight fixture rates for specific cargo types and vessel classes.
- **Monitored Corridor:** A commercial trade corridor registered in the Pelagos database for which genuine, periodic freight market rate observations are ingested and maintained.
- **Unmonitored Corridor:** A trade route or pair of maritime regions for which Pelagos maintains no commercial rate observations or predictive models, regardless of whether a physical navigable sea route exists.
- **Derived Rate Indicator:** A mathematical spread or differential calculated between two directly observed corridors (e.g., `SPREAD-USG-PNW-JPN` = $R_{USG} - R_{PNW}$). Derived indicators cannot be forecast as direct freight rates.
- **Zero-Lookahead Guarantee:** An algorithmic invariant ensuring that a forecast generated for origin date $T$ uses only observations recorded on or before $T$.
- **AIS Telemetry vs. Charter Availability:** AIS provides real-time kinematic position ($lat, lon, speed, course$) of sea-surface vessels. Charter availability refers to commercial open-tonnage status, laycan window compliance, drydock status, and cargo hold readiness. AIS position alone is strictly non-indicative of charter availability.

### 4.2 Route Identity and Endpoint Resolution Rules

1. **Endpoint Resolution Rule:** A route query endpoint may be supplied as a UN/LOCODE string (`USSEA`), port name (`Seattle`), coordinate string (`"-124.75,48.50"`), coordinate list (`[-124.75, 48.50]`), or GeoJSON dictionary.
2. **Commercial Snapping Rule:** If an endpoint is supplied as coordinates, the commercial corridor resolver snaps to the nearest registered port within `max_snap_distance_nm` (configured up to $150.0\text{ NM}$ in `backend/pelagos/routing/corridors.py`). This threshold applies exclusively to commercial endpoint-to-port/region matching. It operates independently of, and must not be conflated with, the $50.0\text{ NM}$ hydrographic entrance-gate snapping threshold defined in Milestone D9.4 ([ROUT-004](./D9.4-stage-3-design.md)), which governs physical sea-lane network entry. If no registered commercial port lies within the $150.0\text{ NM}$ radius, the endpoint is classified as an unmapped regional coordinate.
3. **Regional Mapping Rule:** Ports are mapped to commercial trade regions strictly through an authoritative mapping table (`PORT_REGION_MAP`). Regions are never inferred from latitude/longitude proximity or geometric clustering.
4. **Corridor Matching Rule:** A commercial corridor is resolved if and only if the tuple `(origin_region, destination_region)` matches an entry in `REGISTERED_CORRIDORS`.
5. **Asymmetry Rule:** If `(origin_region, destination_region)` matches a corridor, the reverse tuple `(destination_region, origin_region)` does NOT match unless independently registered. Outbound export fixtures do not apply to inbound backhaul voyages.
6. **No Geometric Fallback:** A route's physical distance, path similarity, or canal transit sequence must NEVER be used to associate it with a commercial corridor.

### 4.3 Commercial Scope and Commodity Boundary

Every monitored corridor is bound to a specific cargo type and vessel class:
- `PNW-JPN-BULK`: Agricultural Bulk Grain, Panamax (60,000–80,000 DWT) / Supramax (50,000–60,000 DWT).
- `USG-JPN-BULK`: Agricultural Bulk Grain, Panamax / Supramax via Panama Canal.
Rates do not represent Capesize iron ore, containerized cargo (TEU spot rates), chemical tankers, or LNG carriers.

### 5.3 Baseline Forecasting & Zero-Lookahead

#### REQ-FR-CRI-006: Route-Conditioned Baseline Rate Forecasting
- **Provenance:** Observed current behavior
- **Maturity:** Implemented & Verified
- **Statement:** For monitored corridors with sufficient historical observations, the system SHALL generate deterministic, explainable baseline point forecasts for a single $T+1$ month forward horizon across supported models: Persistence (naive last value), 3-Month Moving Average (MA-3), 6-Month Moving Average (MA-6), and 6-Month Linear Trend Extrapolation (Trend-6). MA-3 and MA-6 specify historical trailing observation window lengths ($k=3$ and $k=6$ months of prior data) and do not imply three- or six-month-forward forecast horizons. The route intelligence endpoint generates exclusively a 1-month-forward ($T+1$) forecast (`horizon_months: 1`). Forecasts SHALL default to Persistence. Extrapolated trend values falling $\le 0.0$ SHALL be clamped to $0.10\text{ USD/MT}$ with flag `FLAG_TREND_NEGATIVE_CLAMP`.
- **Rationale:** Provides transparent, non-black-box benchmark forecasts against which future machine learning models can be evaluated.
- **Implementation Status:** Implemented in [`backend/pelagos/forecasting/baselines.py`](file:///c:/Users/Lenovo/Desktop/random%20codes/SIH26/Pelagos/backend/pelagos/forecasting/baselines.py) and [`backend/pelagos/forecasting/service.py:258-308`](file:///c:/Users/Lenovo/Desktop/random%20codes/SIH26/Pelagos/backend/pelagos/forecasting/service.py#L258-L308). #### REQ-FR-CRI-007: Strict Zero Future-Data Lookahead Fencing
- **Provenance:** Existing documented commitment
- **Maturity:** Implemented & Verified

### 5.5 Proposed / Unresolved Requirements

#### REQ-FR-CRI-011: Four-State Availability Lifecycle Adaptation
- **Provenance:** Proposed new requirement
- **Maturity:** Proposed (Migration Target)
- **Statement:** The route intelligence service SHALL adapt the four-state forecast availability lifecycle defined in `REQ-FR-FRF-002` while preserving corridor registration (`monitored`/`unmonitored`) and commercial vessel charter availability (`vessel_availability.status: "unavailable"`) as strictly distinct dimensions:
  - `unavailable`: assigned when a corridor cannot support the requested prediction due to physical, geographic, or operational impossibility, accompanied by a specific reason code (`REASON_UNSUPPORTED_GEOGRAPHY` for routes outside catalog scope; `REASON_OPERATIONAL_CLOSURE` for temporary canal, fairway, or terminal closures).
  - `insufficient-data`: assigned when a corridor is supported and operationally open, but observation density, fixture continuity, or feed recency falls below required data-readiness thresholds (`REASON_SPARSE_FIXTURES`, `REASON_OBSERVATION_GAP`, `REASON_STALE_INPUTS`).

Monitored corridors supported only by baseline models without calibrated prediction intervals remain in `eligible`.
  - `available`: assigned ONLY when all required data-readiness and empirical model-validation criteria, including required calibrated prediction intervals on held-out test windows, are satisfied.
- **Rationale:** Aligns future route intelligence lifecycle evolution with governing requirement `REQ-FR-FRF-002`, eliminating the invalid conflation of `monitored` with `available` and ensuring transparent representation of model validation readiness.
- **Implementation Status:** Currently represented as binary `monitored` / `unmonitored` in [`freight.py:284`](file:///c:/Users/Lenovo/Desktop/random%20codes/SIH26/Pelagos/backend/pelagos/api/routes/freight.py#L284). ### 6.1 Primary Historical Freight Ingestion Profile (USDA AMS)

- **Direct Corridors:**
  1. `USG-JPN-BULK`: U.S. Gulf to Japan Bulk Grain Ocean Corridor (Panamax/Supramax via Panama Canal).
  2. `PNW-JPN-BULK`: U.S. Pacific Northwest to Japan Bulk Grain Ocean Corridor (Panamax/Supramax).
- **Derived Indicator:**
  1. `SPREAD-USG-PNW-JPN`: Rate Difference ($R_{USG} - R_{PNW}$). Tagged with `is_derived = true`.

### 6.2 Data Quality & Ingestion Validation Rules

- **Strict Value Boundaries:** Direct freight rates must satisfy $0.0 < rate \le 500.0\text{ USD/MT}$. Non-positive or extreme outlier values are rejected to quarantine ([`freight_usda.py:97`](file:///c:/Users/Lenovo/Desktop/random%20codes/SIH26/Pelagos/backend/pelagos/ingestion/freight_usda.py#L97)).
- **Zero Synthetic Interpolation:** Missing calendar months are recorded as gaps. No linear interpolation, spline fitting, or backfilling is permitted ([REQ-NFR-DIP-001](../requirements/nonfunctional-requirements.md)).
- **Deduplication:** Enforced via composite unique constraint `(route_code, observation_date)` and deterministic SHA-256 idempotency key `source:route_code:date`.

## 8. Prediction Eligibility and Insufficient-Data Behavior

### 8.1 Disaggregation of Readiness Concepts

To eliminate conceptual confusion, Pelagos strictly disaggregates four independent dimensions of readiness:

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    DISAGGREGATED READINESS DIMENSIONS                                   |
+-------------------+----------------------------+----------------------------+---------------------------+
| 1. Corridor       | 2. Observation             | 3. Forecast                | 4. Commercial Charter     |
|    Registration   |    Availability            |    Eligibility             |    Availability           |
+-------------------+----------------------------+----------------------------+---------------------------+
| Is the trade lane | Do genuine historical rate | Has the corridor passed    | Are candidate open-tonnage|
| registered in the | observations exist in      | minimum data density and   | vessels physically and    |
| commercial trade  | SQLite/Parquet storage for | demonstrated out-of-sample | contractually available   |
| catalog?          | this corridor?             | predictive validity?       | for laycan fixing?        |
|                   |                            |                            |                           |
| [corridors.py]    | [FreightObservation]       | [REQ-FR-FRF-002, ML-001]   | [D10 Chartering Engine]   |
+-------------------+----------------------------+----------------------------+---------------------------+
```

Corridor registration (`monitored`/`unmonitored`) indicates whether a trade lane is cataloged in Pelagos. Forecast lifecycle state (`unavailable`, `insufficient-data`, `eligible`, `available` per `REQ-FR-FRF-002`) measures data sufficiency and empirical model validation. Commercial charter availability measures open-tonnage contract readiness. These three dimensions are strictly orthogonal and must never be conflated.

### 8.2 Prediction Eligibility & Insufficient-Data Rules

1. **Sufficient History Gate:** Baseline forecasting requires a minimum number of valid historical observations preceding the origin date:
   - Persistence: $\ge 1$ observation.
   - Rolling MA-3: $\ge 3$ consecutive historical observations (historical 3-month averaging window).
   - Rolling MA-6 / Trend-6: $\ge 6$ consecutive historical observations (historical 6-month window).
   The engine generates exclusively a $T+1$ month forward forecast (`horizon_months: 1`). If history falls below the required window threshold, the engine returns `status: "insufficient_history"`, `predicted_value: null`, and attaches an explanatory note.
2. **Unsupported Route Rejection:** Routes not registered in `REGISTERED_CORRIDORS` are ineligible for forecasting.
3. **Derived Spread Rejection:** Derived indicators (`SPREAD-USG-PNW-JPN`) are rejected with HTTP 400 if queried for direct rate prediction.

## 9. Freshness, Uncertainty, and Historical-Data Labeling

### 9.1 Data Freshness & Staleness Boundaries

- **Monthly Anchor:** Observations represent monthly market averages anchored to the first day of each month (`YYYY-MM-01`).
- **Staleness Indication:** Because freight market feeds publish on monthly cycles, observations remain fresh for up to 45 calendar days following publication. Observations older than 60 calendar days trigger an informational notice in the UI regarding delayed market feed updates.

### 9.2 Uncertainty and Prediction Intervals

- **Current Baseline Limitation:** Point forecasts generated by baseline models (Persistence, MA, Trend) represent single expected values without confidence bands.
- **Provisional Calibration Targets:** In accordance with `REQ-FR-FRF-001` and `ML-001`, future econometric and machine learning models will be required to demonstrate $\ge 80\%$ empirical coverage for nominal 80% (p10–p90) prediction intervals and $\ge 95\%$ coverage for nominal 95% intervals on held-out test windows before promotion to active production.

## 12. Security, Reliability, and Operational Constraints

- **Core Technology Independence:** Pure Python, DuckDB, and NumPy implementation. Zero external proprietary API keys (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Cloud AI) are required or permitted ([REQ-NFR-DCT-001](../requirements/nonfunctional-requirements.md)).
- **Read-Only Engine Safety:** Querying route intelligence or calculating forecasts is strictly read-only and SHALL NOT mutate SQLite tables or Parquet partition files.
- **Fail-Closed Execution:** Malformed coordinates, invalid route codes, or database connection failures fail closed with structured error responses rather than returning corrupted or defaulted numeric values.
- **Memory Footprint:** Graph loading and DuckDB connections are instantiated on-demand and released cleanly, preventing memory leaks during long-running daemon execution.

## 14. Risks, Unresolved Decisions, and Change History

### 14.1 Open Decisions Requiring Project Owner Approval

1. **OD-ROUT-06-01: Reconciliation of Forecasting Horizons (Daily vs. Monthly) — Resolved (Approved):**
   - *Resolution:* Formally approved by Project Owner on 2026-10-24 ([OD-ROUT-06-01](./od-rout-06-01-forecasting-horizons-amendment-proposal.md)). Reconciled `REQ-FR-FRF-001` to establish discrete calendar-month evaluation horizons ($T+1, T+3, T+6$ months) for datasets sampled at monthly cadence. Daily forecast horizons are strictly quarantined outside the monthly data scope and may only be considered under a separate requirements change supported by genuine daily observations. Established explicit temporal semantics (observation period, data availability time, forecast execution time, forecast origin, and target period). Candidate horizons represent design targets and do not imply active production capability; model promotion remains strictly gated by out-of-sample empirical evaluation under `REQ-FR-FRF-002`. The current D9.5 route-intelligence endpoint continues to generate exclusively a 1-month-forward ($T+1$) persistence baseline (`horizon_months: 1`), with API query parameters and mid-month conventions deferred to future design.
2. **OD-ROUT-06-02: REST API Lifecycle State Migration (Binary vs. 4-State):**
   - *Discrepancy:* `REQ-FR-FRF-002` mandates a 4-state lifecycle (`unavailable`, `insufficient-data`, `eligible`, `available`). The current D9.5 route-intelligence API uses `status: "monitored" | "unmonitored"`.
   - *Analysis:* Equating `monitored` with `available` is invalid under `REQ-FR-FRF-002`, because `available` is strictly reserved for models that have passed the formal empirical out-of-sample evaluation gate against reference baselines with calibrated prediction intervals. Current baseline models (Persistence, MA-3, MA-6, Trend-6) lack calibrated prediction intervals and out-of-sample gate certification; hence, in `REQ-FR-FRF-002` terms, monitored corridors with sufficient data are at most `eligible` (`REASON_PENDING_MODEL_VALIDATION`), while unmonitored routes are `unavailable` (`REASON_UNSUPPORTED_GEOGRAPHY`).
   - *Recommendation:* Evaluate introducing an additive `lifecycle_state` field in `RouteIntelligenceResponse` in a future milestone, preserving `status: "monitored" | "unmonitored"` for corridor catalog registration while mapping lifecycle states strictly in accordance with `REQ-FR-FRF-002`:
     - `unavailable`: assigned when the route/corridor cannot support prediction due to physical, geographic, or operational impossibility (e.g., `REASON_UNSUPPORTED_GEOGRAPHY` for out-of-scope/unregistered routes; `REASON_OPERATIONAL_CLOSURE` for temporary canal or terminal disruptions).
     - `insufficient-data`: assigned when observations do not meet required data-readiness sufficiency thresholds (e.g., `REASON_SPARSE_FIXTURES`, `REASON_OBSERVATION_GAP`, `REASON_STALE_INPUTS`).
     - `eligible`: assigned when data-readiness criteria are satisfied (e.g., 24-month observation history exists), but the model has not passed the formal empirical predictive evaluation gate with calibrated prediction intervals (`REASON_PENDING_MODEL_VALIDATION`), or has experienced performance drift (`REASON_MODEL_DRIFT_DEGRADATION`).
     - `available`: reserved strictly for active models satisfying both data readiness and formal empirical predictive evaluation gates on genuine held-out data with calibrated prediction intervals.
     Corridor registration (`monitored` / `unmonitored`) remains a separate structural catalog dimension, and commercial vessel charter availability (`vessel_availability.status: "unavailable"`) remains strictly separated from both. This proposal remains an open decision pending Project Owner authorization and does not authorize changes to current API schemas or response payloads.
3. **OD-ROUT-06-03: Expansion of Port-to-Region Directory:**
   - *Limitation:* `PORT_REGION_MAP` currently hardcodes only 8 UN/LOCODEs. Other major Pacific Northwest grain loading ports (e.g., Portland `USPDX`, Kalama `USKAL`) do not resolve to `US Pacific Northwest`.
   - *Recommendation:* Author a database-backed port-to-region mapping table in Milestone D9.5 Increment 2 covering all major grain loading and discharge terminals.
4. **OD-ROUT-06-04: Additional Historical Freight Data Sourcing:**
   - *Limitation:* Only two corridors (`PNW-JPN-BULK`, `USG-JPN-BULK`) have genuine rate observations.
   - *Recommendation:* Formalize licensing and ingestion for public or commercial datasets (e.g., UNCTAD Maritime Transport indicators, World Bank Pink Sheet bulk shipping data, or Baltic Dry Index fixtures) before claiming expanded geographic corridor coverage.
